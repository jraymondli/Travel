I think Mexico City is an excellent choice for your first trip together to Mexico. It's especially appealing if you enjoy great food, walking through interesting neighborhoods, historic architecture, museums, and a relaxed evening over drinks.

For five days, I recommend staying in one hotel in Roma Norte or Polanco, spending three days exploring the city, and taking one excursion to the ancient pyramids of Teotihuacán.

November is generally a very pleasant month: mild afternoons, cooler evenings, and relatively little rain.

## 2. Entry requirements: Chinese passports + U.S. green cards

Good news: You should not need a Mexican visa.

The Mexican Consulate in San Jose explicitly states that U.S. permanent residents traveling to Mexico need a valid green card and a valid, unexpired passport. This applies to you and your wife even though you both hold Chinese passports.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx\&sz=32)

Consulado de México



Before booking, make sure you each have:

- A valid Chinese passport, preferably with at least six months remaining.
- Your original, unexpired U.S. permanent resident card (I-551).
- Your return flight itinerary and hotel reservation.
- A passport and green card ready to present when boarding and entering Mexico, plus the green card for reentry into the U.S.

For travelers arriving by air, Mexico now generally generates immigration records digitally rather than requiring a paper FMM.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.inm.gob.mx\&sz=32)

Instituto Nacional de Migracion



If either of your green cards is expired and you're relying on an extension notice, check directly with the Mexican consulate and airline before booking; the simple visa exemption may not apply in the same way.

## 6. Practical advice for your first visit

- Altitude: Mexico City sits about 2,240 meters (7,350 feet) above sea level. Take it easy on arrival, hydrate, and don't be surprised if walking uphill feels harder.

- Weather: Expect daytime temperatures around 70–75°F, with cooler nights around 45–50°F. Bring a light jacket and comfortable walking shoes.

- Transportation: Use Uber or hotel-arranged transport, especially after dark. I would not rent a car.

- Safety: Stick to established tourist neighborhoods and use ordinary city precautions. The U.S. State Department advises increased caution in Mexico City because of crime.&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://travel.state.gov\&sz=32)

  Travel.state.gov

- Food: Try street food at busy, reputable stalls, but drink bottled or properly filtered water.
