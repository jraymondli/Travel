Sept 5: Calgary → Banff

Calgary airport to Banff is roughly 1½–2 hours, so unless your flight arrives very late, I’d drive directly to Banff rather than spend the first night in Calgary.

Keep this day light: check in, walk Banff Avenue, have dinner, perhaps stroll along the Bow River.

Sept 6: Banff proper

I’d use one full day for the Banff area rather than rushing straight to Lake Louise.

Good combination: Lake Minnewanka → Two Jack Lake → Bow Falls → Surprise Corner, then perhaps Banff Gondola/Sulphur Mountain late afternoon.

If you like moderate hiking, you can substitute a hike for the gondola.

Sept 7: Lake Louise + Moraine Lake

This is one of the trip’s most important days.

I would actually sleep at Lake Louise this night, rather than returning to Banff. It positions you perfectly for the Icefields Parkway the next morning.

For 2026, private cars cannot drive to Moraine Lake. Parks Canada shuttle reservations are required. One shuttle reservation gives you transportation to both Lake Louise and Moraine Lake via the connector. Forty percent of seats are already released, while the remaining 60% are released at 8 a.m. Mountain Time two days before the visit. 

So I’d try to reserve September 7 now. If sold out, set an alarm for 8 a.m. MT September 5 for the last-minute release.

And September 7 has a little bonus: Parks Canada’s Canada Strong Pass promotion gives free national-park admission through September 7. Starting September 8 you’ll need a normal park pass. 

Sept 8: Lake Louise → Jasper via Icefields Parkway

This shouldn’t be treated as a 3½-hour drive. Give it essentially the whole day.

I’d stop at roughly:

Bow Lake → Peyto Lake → Saskatchewan River Crossing → Columbia Icefield/Athabasca Glacier → Sunwapta Falls → Athabasca Falls → Jasper.

This is likely to be one of the highlights of the entire trip.

You don’t need to stop everywhere on the northbound journey because you’ll travel the parkway again on September 11. That gives you flexibility for weather, which is valuable in September.

Sept 9: Jasper / lighter day

After the long sightseeing drive, make this a relatively relaxed day.

I like:

Pyramid Lake + Pyramid Island → Patricia Lake → Jasper town → Lac Beauvert, plus either Valley of the Five Lakes or another moderate hike.

Valley of the Five Lakes has reopened for 2026, although one small trail segment remains closed. Most of Jasper’s trails have reopened following the 2024 wildfire. 

Sept 10: Maligne Lake

Spend most of this day along Maligne Lake Road:

Medicine Lake → Maligne Lake → boat cruise to Spirit Island if that appeals to you.

Maligne Lake itself and its trailhead/day-use area are open in 2026. 

One important change from older itineraries you’ll see online: Maligne Canyon is closed for the entire 2026 season, and so is Edith Cavell Road/Cavell Meadows, because of ongoing wildfire recovery work. Don’t build either into your itinerary. 

Sept 11: Jasper → Banff/Canmore

Drive the Icefields Parkway south again.

Normally I dislike backtracking, but here I think it is a feature rather than a problem. The road is spectacular in both directions, and it lets you save some stops for the return trip.

For example, if you did Peyto Lake and Columbia Icefield going north, stop at Sunwapta Falls, Athabasca Falls, Parker Ridge or Bow Lake on the return.

I’d spend this night in Banff or Canmore. Canmore is often less expensive and gives you a different atmosphere.

Sept 12: leisurely final day → Calgary

Don’t schedule another huge sightseeing day.

Have breakfast in Banff/Canmore, perhaps do something you missed, then drive to Calgary. Spend your final night near downtown Calgary or the airport depending on the September 13 flight time.

I strongly prefer this to sleeping in Jasper on the 12th and facing a 400+ km drive to Calgary on flight day. Jasper is about 404 km from Calgary, and mountain driving can be slowed by weather, traffic or wildlife. 

A few things I’d book soon

The trip is only about two weeks away, so I’d prioritize rental car, Jasper lodging, Lake Louise-area lodging, and the Lake Louise/Moraine Lake shuttle. Jasper accommodations in particular can constrain the whole itinerary.

Also, early September is excellent timing: generally fewer crowds than July/August, long enough daylight, and potentially the beginning of fall color. But mountain weather can change quickly, so bring layers and don’t make the itinerary dependent on perfect conditions. Parks Canada specifically recommends checking Alberta 511 for current Jasper-area road conditions rather than relying solely on navigation apps.
