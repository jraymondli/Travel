For **TMB with luggage transfer**, I’d frame it this way:

- **WoneNice 50L at $41:** very good budget value. It has plenty of space, includes a rain cover, and is more than capable of carrying your day gear.
- **Osprey/Gregory around $100–130+:** you’re mainly paying for **better suspension, ventilation, fit, hip-belt comfort, durability, and warranty**, not because you need more storage.

The main downside of the WoneNice isn’t that it won’t work. It’s that **50L is much larger than you need**, and its harness system is more basic. On a 6–8 hour hiking day, a better-fitting 25–30L pack can feel noticeably more comfortable, especially on long climbs and descents.

For your trip, though, you’ll probably carry only something like **4–6 kg** most days. At that weight, the WoneNice’s simpler suspension is less of a concern than it would be if you were carrying 10–15 kg.

So I’d actually suggest this:

**If you already own the WoneNice, use it and don’t buy another pack yet.** Load it with about **10–12 lb (4.5–5.5 kg)** and take it on a couple of **5–8 mile hikes with hills**. If your shoulders, lower back, and hips still feel good after 3–4 hours, there’s no compelling reason to spend another $100+.

If you’re buying from scratch, the decision becomes **$41 WoneNice vs roughly $100+ Osprey**. For occasional hiking and one TMB trip, I’d be quite tempted by the **$41 WoneNice**. For frequent hiking over the next several years, I’d spend more on the Osprey.

One thing I would do with the 50L pack: **don’t fill it just because the space is there.** Treat it like a 25–30L daypack and keep your TMB day load around **5 kg or less**. That matters more than the nominal backpack capacity.
